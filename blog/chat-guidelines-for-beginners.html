Chat Guidelines for Beginners: How to Have Better Conversations (and Earn More)

Key Takeaways

  • Great chats = repeat clients. The people who come back are worth 3–5x more than one-time chatters.
  • Never open with "Hi." Ask something specific about their profile or country — it changes everything.
  • Use open-ended questions. Questions starting with "what," "how," or "tell me" get real answers.
  • Be genuinely curious. People can feel when you're just chasing money versus actually listening.
  • End warm, always. A great goodbye is the #1 way to get a second conversation.

1 Why Conversation Quality = Income

When new chatters start on MegaChat, most of them focus on one thing: how many chats can I get? That's the wrong question. The right question is: how good can I make each chat?

Here's the math. A one-time chatter who talks to you for 10 minutes might earn you $2–$5. But a repeat client who comes back every week and chats for an hour? That's $20–$50 per session, every week, for months. Over a year, one great repeat client can be worth more than 100 random chats.

And here's the thing about repeat clients: they don't come back for the topic. They come back for the feeling. The way you made them laugh, the story you told about Nairobi, the fact that you remembered their daughter's name from last week. Those things can't be faked. They come from genuinely good conversations.

"My best clients aren't the ones who tip the most. They're the ones who come back because they actually enjoy talking to me. Once you have five of those, your income stops being about effort and starts being about consistency."

— Amaka N., top MegaChat earner

The good news? Great conversation is a skill, not a talent. You can learn it. And in this guide, we're going to give you the exact tools that top earners use every day.

2 How to Open a Chat (Not Just "Hi")

Your opening message is the most important one you'll send. It's the difference between a real conversation and a dead one. Most beginners write something like:

  • "Hi"
  • "Hello, how are you?"
  • "What's up?"

These are conversation killers. They're vague, they require effort from the other person, and they don't give anyone anything to respond to. A stranger will either ignore them or reply with "fine, you?" and then the chat dies.

What to do instead

Open with something specific, warm, and easy to respond to. Use this simple formula:

The 3-part opener: "Hi [name]! I noticed you're from [place / mentioned X] — [specific, curious question about it]."

Here are some real examples:

  • "Hi Margaret! I saw you're from Toronto — I've always wanted to visit. What's one thing I should absolutely do there?"
  • "Hi Robert! I saw in your bio that you love jazz. Who's your favourite artist? I'm trying to learn more."
  • "Hi Helga! Your profile says you've travelled a lot — what's the most surprising place you've ever been?"

Notice what these have in common: they're specific, they show you actually read the profile, and they invite a story — not a yes/no answer.

3 12 Questions That Always Work

These are the questions top earners use over and over — not because they're magic, but because they consistently open up real conversations. Save them, adapt them, and use them naturally.

1
What's something you've always wanted to try but haven't yet?
2
What's a small thing that made you smile today?
3
If you could live anywhere in the world for a year, where would you go?
4
What's the best meal you've had recently?
5
What's a song that always puts you in a good mood?
6
What's something most people don't know about you?
7
What's a place you've visited that surprised you?
8
What's something you're really good at that you don't talk about much?
9
What's a hobby you've always wanted to learn?
10
What's the best advice you've ever received?
11
If you could have dinner with anyone, living or not, who would it be?
12
What's something you're looking forward to this month?

Important: don't fire these off like a script. Pick one that fits the moment, ask it, and then actually listen to the answer. Follow up. Share your own answer. That's how you turn a question into a conversation.

4 4 Phrases That Keep Chats Alive

Sometimes a conversation goes quiet. It's not because the person is bored — it's because they don't know what to say next. These four phrases give them an easy way back in:

"Tell me more about that — I'm curious."
Invites them to expand. Shows you're actually listening, not just waiting to speak.
"That reminds me of something…"
Shares a small story about yourself. Keeps the exchange balanced — not an interview.
"I've never thought about it that way — what made you see it like that?"
Makes the other person feel genuinely interesting, which they'll remember.
"How did that feel?"
The most powerful question in conversation. People love sharing how things felt, not just what happened.

5 3 Habits of Top Earners

We've watched the highest-earning chatters on MegaChat — the ones consistently making $1,500+ per month. They share three habits that anyone can copy:

1. They remember names and details

Top earners keep simple notes about their repeat clients — the name of their daughter, the city they grew up in, their favourite football team. When they say "How did your daughter's recital go?" three weeks later, the client feels genuinely seen. That's what keeps them coming back.

2. They bring warmth, not desperation

Clients can feel the difference between someone who's genuinely enjoying the conversation and someone who's just counting minutes. Top earners are warm, playful, and relaxed. They treat the chat as a real conversation that happens to be paid — not a transaction they're trying to squeeze.

3. They always end with a great goodbye

This is the underrated habit. Top earners never just disappear. They say something like: "This was such a lovely chat — I really enjoyed hearing about your trip. I hope you have a wonderful week, and I'd love to talk again soon." That's it. That's how you get a second conversation.

6 Mistakes That Kill Conversations

These are the most common mistakes we see from beginners. Avoid all of them:

  • Asking yes/no questions. "Do you like music?" gets "yes." "What kind of music do you love?" gets a real conversation.
  • Making it feel like an interview. If you're the only one asking questions, the chat will die. Share your own answers, tell stories, be a participant — not just a question machine.
  • Rushing to money topics. Don't bring up payment, gifts, or earnings in the chat. It kills the mood instantly and makes clients feel used.
  • Writing in one-word replies. "lol", "ok", "cool" — these are conversation enders, not continuers. Even a short sentence like "oh that's so true, I've felt that" is better.
  • Bringing up politics, religion, or personal finances. These topics get heavy fast and can offend. Stick to hobbies, travel, food, culture, music, memories — the light, warm stuff.
  • Being negative. Complaining about your day, your country, or your life drains the conversation. Clients come to you for warmth and lightness — not to be your therapist.
  • Disappearing without saying goodbye. If you have to leave, say so. It's basic respect, and clients remember it.

One more critical rule: never ask for gifts or money in a chat. If a client offers, you can accept warmly — but asking is the fastest way to lose a repeat client. Be gracious, not greedy.

7 Frequently Asked Questions

What should I say to start a conversation on MegaChat?

Start with something specific and warm, not generic. Instead of "Hi," try "Hi! I saw you're from Canada — I've always wanted to visit. What's your favourite city there?" Specific questions get real answers.

How do I keep a conversation going when it goes quiet?

Ask an open-ended question about something they mentioned earlier, share a small story about yourself, or use a fun question like "What's something you've always wanted to try but haven't yet?" The key is to give them something easy to respond to.

What topics should I avoid when chatting online?

Avoid politics, religion, personal finance details, and anything that feels intrusive. Stick to hobbies, travel, food, culture, music, and light personal stories. Keep it warm and respectful.

How do I turn a first chat into a repeat client?

End every conversation warmly, remember their name and something they told you, and invite them to chat again. Repeat clients are the biggest driver of long-term earnings on MegaChat.

Should I use a script for my chats?

You can use these guidelines as a starting point, but never read from a script. Clients can feel it instantly. Learn the structure, then be yourself. The best chatters sound like friends, not customer service reps.

How long should a chat last?

There's no fixed length. Some great chats last 10 minutes; others go for hours. The right length is however long it feels natural and enjoyable — for both of you. Never drag a chat just to earn more. It shows.
